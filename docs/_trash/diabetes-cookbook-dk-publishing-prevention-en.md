## Low GI Diet Principles

This book emphasizes low GI diet principles—the core of diabetes patient dietary management. GI (Glycemic Index) is a measure of how quickly foods raise blood sugar. Low GI foods are digested and absorbed more slowly, don't cause rapid blood sugar spikes, and are ideal for diabetes patients.

Low GI foods mainly include: most vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, etc. High GI foods include: white rice, white flour, bread, potatoes, sweets, etc. The book recommends replacing refined rice and flour with whole grains, substituting legumes for some staple foods, and increasing vegetable intake.

Besides choosing low GI foods, food pairing is also important. Protein and fat can slow carbohydrate absorption and reduce post-meal blood sugar spikes. The book recommends combining protein, vegetables, and healthy fats at each meal.

## Quick Breakfast Recipes

Breakfast is the most important meal of the day, especially for diabetes patients. Here are some simple and easy breakfast recipes:

**Oatmeal**: Use traditional oatmeal, cook with water or low-fat milk, topped with nuts and berries. Oats are rich in soluble fiber, which can slow blood sugar rise.

**Vegetable Omelet**: Beat eggs, add spinach, tomatoes, mushrooms, etc., and make into an omelet. Rich in protein, vegetables provide fiber—perfect breakfast choice.

**Whole Wheat Toast with Avocado**: Whole wheat bread topped with half an avocado, seasoned with salt and pepper. Avocado is rich in healthy fats, providing lasting satiety.

**Greek Yogurt Bowl**: Low-fat Greek yogurt topped with nuts, seeds, and fresh fruit. High protein content, minimal blood sugar impact.

## Nutritious Lunch Bento

For diabetes patients who need to bring lunch to work, here are some recipes suitable for bento boxes:

**Quinoa Vegetable Salad**: Cooked quinoa mixed with cucumber, tomatoes, chickpeas, and feta cheese, drizzled with olive oil and lemon juice. Quinoa is a complete protein, vegetables provide fiber—a balanced nutritional choice.

**Chicken Vegetable Skewers**: Chicken breast cut into chunks with bell peppers, onions, cherry tomatoes, skewered and grilled or pan-fried. Rich in protein, vegetables add fiber.

**Cauliflower Fried Rice**: Using cauliflower "rice" instead of white rice, paired with tofu, eggs, and vegetables. A low-carbohydrate alternative to fried rice.

**Italian Vegetable Soup**: Thick soup made with tomatoes, carrots, celery, and kidney beans. Rich in fiber, low in carbohydrates.

## Cozy Dinner Recipes

Dinner is family gathering time. Here are some healthy dinner recipes suitable for the whole family:

**Pan-Seared Salmon with Asparagus**: Pan-sear salmon until golden on both sides, served with steamed asparagus. Salmon is rich in Omega-3 fatty acids, beneficial for cardiovascular health.

**Chicken Broccoli Stir-Fry**: Sliced chicken breast quick-fried with broccoli, seasoned with a small amount of soy sauce. Perfect combination of protein and vegetables.

**Beef and Vegetable Stew**: Beef chunks with carrots, potatoes, and kidney beans slow-cooked until tender. Tender meat, flavorful vegetables—perfect for cold nights.

**Shrimp Vegetable Noodles**: Stir-fried with bean sprouts, greens, shrimp, and a small amount of low-sodium soy sauce. Shrimp is high-quality protein, vegetables add fiber.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q1: Can diabetes patients eat fruit?

Yes. Choose fruits with low GI values, such as berries, apples, pears, oranges, etc. It is recommended to eat fruit between meals, not immediately after meals.

### Q2: Can sugar-free foods be eaten freely?

Sugar-free doesn't mean no calories or won't raise blood sugar. Many sugar-free foods still contain large amounts of fat and carbohydrates—intake needs to be controlled.

### Q3: Do diabetes patients need to completely avoid carbohydrates?

No. Carbohydrates are an important energy source—the key is choosing the correct type and appropriate amount. Choosing complex carbohydrates like whole grains and legumes is more beneficial for blood sugar control than refined carbohydrates.
