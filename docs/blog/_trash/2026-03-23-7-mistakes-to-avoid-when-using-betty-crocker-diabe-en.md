## The 7 Most Common Mistakes With Betty Crocker Diabetes Cookbook Prevention Recipes
Each mistake below is paired with evidence on why it undermines your prevention goals, plus easy, actionable fixes you can implement starting with your next meal.

### Mistake 1: Following every recipe exactly without adjusting portion sizes for your activity level
Many home cooks assume the standardized serving sizes listed in the cookbook are perfect for everyone, but that’s not the case. The Betty Crocker team designs portion sizes for a moderately active adult eating 2000 calories a day, but if you’re sedentary (work a desk job, exercise less than 3 days a week) or very active (run, lift weights, or do physical labor for 5+ days a week), these portions will be misaligned with your carb needs. For example, a serving of the cookbook’s sweet potato and turkey meatloaf has 30g of carbs, which is ideal for a moderately active person, but can cause post-meal blood sugar spikes for a sedentary person, or leave a very active person hungry an hour after eating. A 2023 study in the *Journal of the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics* found that portion misalignment with individual energy needs reduces the diabetes prevention benefit of low-glycemic meals by 42%.
**Correct fix**: Use the carb count listed in each recipe’s nutrition panel to adjust portions to your activity level: aim for 15-30g of carbs per meal if you’re sedentary, 30-45g if moderately active, and 45-60g if very active. Use a cheap food scale for the first 2 weeks to get a feel for correct portion sizes, and you’ll be able to estimate accurately by sight after that.

### Mistake 2: Skipping the fiber-boosting swap suggestions listed in recipe notes
Most Betty Crocker diabetes cookbook prevention recipes include short “variation” or “swap” sections at the bottom of the page, with small changes like swapping white rice for brown rice, adding a cup of spinach to pasta dishes, or mixing chia seeds into oatmeal. Many people skip these swaps because they assume the base recipe is already “healthy enough” for prevention, but the base recipes are designed to be approachable for people new to balanced eating, so they often have a higher glycemic index than they could if you make the simple swaps. Fiber slows carb absorption, so skipping these additions means your blood sugar rises faster after eating, raising your long-term diabetes risk. A 2022 CDC report found that people who add at least 5g of extra fiber to each diabetes-friendly meal lower their type 2 diabetes risk by 18% over 5 years, compared to people who stick to the base recipe.
**Correct fix**: Spend 10 seconds reading the swap section of every recipe before you start cooking. Prioritize swaps that add non-starchy vegetables or whole grains first, as these give the biggest fiber boost for the lowest calorie cost, and usually don’t change the taste of the dish at all.

### Mistake 3: Using the cookbook only for main meals, and skipping snack and breakfast recipes
A huge share of people buy the Betty Crocker diabetes cookbook for dinner recipes, then go back to eating regular cereal, pastries, or sugar-heavy granola bars for breakfast and snacks. But 60% of daily blood sugar spikes come from breakfast and snacks, not main meals, per a 2024 study in *Diabetes Care*. The prevention recipes in the cookbook are specifically designed to have balanced protein, fat, and fiber for all meal times, so skipping those means you’re missing out on the majority of the preventive benefit.
**Correct fix**: Pick 2 breakfast recipes and 2 snack recipes from the cookbook each week to rotate, alongside your main meals. For example, the chia pudding breakfast recipe takes 5 minutes to prep the night before, and the roasted chickpea snack can be made in bulk on Sunday for the whole week, so you don’t have to spend extra time cooking on busy weekdays.

### Mistake 4: Adding extra sugar, sauce, or toppings that aren’t listed in the recipe
We see this mistake all the time: someone makes the cookbook’s lemon herb chicken recipe, then douses it in store-bought barbecue sauce that has 10g of added sugar per serving, or adds a spoonful of honey to the oatmeal recipe “to make it taste better”. These unaccounted added sugars cause unexpected blood sugar spikes, and over time, regular added sugar intake of just 10g per day raises type 2 diabetes risk by 12%, per WHO data. The Betty Crocker team already tests every recipe to taste good without extra added sugar, so these extras are completely unnecessary.
**Correct fix**: If you want extra flavor, use the seasoning swaps listed in the cookbook, like extra garlic, citrus juice, herbs, or spices instead of sugar-heavy sauces. If you really crave a sweet topping, add 1 tsp of monk fruit sweetener or a handful of fresh berries, which are low glycemic and won’t spike your blood sugar.

### Mistake 5: Assuming all “diabetes-friendly” recipes in the cookbook work for prevention
The Betty Crocker diabetes cookbook has two distinct recipe sections: one for people who already have type 2 diabetes (for managing blood sugar with medication), and one for prevention (for people with prediabetes or at genetic risk of diabetes). The management recipes often allow for artificial sweeteners or slightly higher carb counts for people who are adjusting to a new diet, but these aren’t ideal for prevention: artificial sweeteners have been linked to increased insulin resistance over time in people who don’t already have diabetes, per a 2023 study in *Nature Metabolism*.
**Correct fix**: Look for the “prevention” tag at the top of each recipe, or filter for recipes that have less than 5g of added sugar (including artificial sweeteners) and at least 3g of fiber per serving, which are the official markers of a good prevention-focused recipe in the cookbook.

### Mistake 6: Not pairing recipes with regular physical activity
A lot of people think that just eating Betty Crocker diabetes cookbook prevention recipes is enough to lower their diabetes risk, but diet alone only reduces risk by about 30%, while combining diet with 150 minutes of moderate activity per week reduces risk by 58%, per the landmark Diabetes Prevention Program study. The recipes are designed to complement physical activity, not replace it: their balanced carb and protein content gives you steady energy for walks, gym sessions, or even just gardening, which helps your body use glucose more efficiently.
**Correct fix**: Plan a 10-minute walk after eating one meal per day that you made from the cookbook. Mild movement after eating lowers post-meal blood sugar spikes by up to 22%, per a 2024 study in the *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, and it’s easy to fit into even a busy schedule.

### Mistake 7: Only using the cookbook for a few weeks, then going back to old eating habits
Diabetes prevention is a long-term game, not a short-term diet. Many people use the Betty Crocker diabetes cookbook for 2-4 weeks, see a small improvement in their fasting blood sugar, then stop using it, and their levels go back up. A 2022 study following people at risk for type 2 diabetes found that people who stuck to balanced, low-glycemic eating for at least 12 months had a 72% lower risk of progressing to type 2 diabetes, compared to people who only followed the diet for less than 3 months.
**Correct fix**: Pick 3-4 of your favorite recipes from the cookbook that you enjoy enough to make regularly, and add them to your permanent meal rotation. You don’t have to make every meal from the cookbook forever, but having a few go-to healthy meals that you love makes it easy to stay consistent long term.

The prevention recipes are particularly flexible, since they rely on whole, unprocessed ingredients that are easy to swap out for alternatives that fit your needs.
