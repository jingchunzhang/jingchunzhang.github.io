### 1. Portability and Durability
Spring means you’ll spend more time away from home, so look for monitors that weigh less than 2 ounces, fit in a pocket or fanny pack, and are water and scratch resistant. For continuous glucose monitors (CGMs), prioritize options with strong adhesive that stays in place if you sweat during hikes or get caught in spring rain.

### 2. Data Sync and Real-Time Alerts
If you’re testing new seasonal foods or adjusting your workout routine for spring, look for products that sync to a mobile app to log glucose levels alongside food and activity entries. Real-time high/low glucose alerts are especially valuable during busy holiday gatherings or long hikes, where you might not remember to check your levels manually.

### 3. Total Cost (Upfront + Recurring)
Budget monitors often have low upfront costs but expensive test strips, so calculate your annual cost before buying. If you test 2 times per day, a pack of 50 test strips per month adds up: a $17 per 50 strip option costs $204 per year, while a $30 per 50 strip option costs $360 per year. For CGMs, check if your insurance covers part or all of the cost before purchasing out of pocket.

### 4. Weather Resilience
Spring temperatures can fluctuate 20-30 degrees in a single day, so look for monitors that work reliably in temperatures between 32°F and 104°F. Avoid monitors that require very specific storage temperatures, as they may give inaccurate readings if left in a hot car or cold hiking pack for hours.

### 5. Widespread Availability
If you travel frequently during spring, pick a brand with test strips and replacement parts available at most national pharmacy and grocery store chains, so you don’t get stuck without supplies if you run out away from home.

## FAQ (Common Questions About Glucose During Spring)
### Q: Do spring allergies affect my glucose levels?
A: Yes. The cortisol released during allergic reactions can raise glucose by 20-40 points for 6-12 hours, even if you don’t change your diet or exercise routine. If you take antihistamines, opt for sugar-free versions, as many liquid antihistamines have added sugar that can cause additional spikes.

### Q: Can I wear my CGM while swimming or doing spring water activities?
A: Most modern CGMs (including the Freestyle Libre 2 and Dexcom G7) are water resistant for up to 30-60 minutes in shallow water. For longer swims or water sports, add a waterproof adhesive patch over the sensor to keep it secure. Avoid submerging CGMs in hot tubs, as high temperatures can damage the sensor.

### Q: What spring seasonal foods should I watch out for to avoid glucose spikes?
A: Sweet rice cakes (Lunar New Year), mangoes, honey-glazed ham, sweetened iced teas, lemon bars, and potato salad all have high glycemic loads that can cause large glucose spikes. Pair these foods with protein or fiber (like nuts, chicken, or leafy greens) to reduce the rate of sugar absorption and minimize spikes.

### Q: Do I need to adjust my glucose monitoring routine in spring?
A: If you’re eating more seasonal foods, traveling, or exercising more outdoors, we recommend increasing your monitoring frequency by 1-2 times per day, or switching to a CGM to track fluctuations in real time. This will help you identify patterns specific to your spring routine and make adjustments to keep your levels stable.
