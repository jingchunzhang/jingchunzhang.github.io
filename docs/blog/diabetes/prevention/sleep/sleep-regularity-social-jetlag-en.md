# Sleep Regularity Matters: Social Jet Lag and Metabolic Risk

> **[中文版](./sleep-regularity-social-jetlag.md)**

Most sleep conversations start with “get 7–9 hours.” But another variable is often just as practical:

> **Do you sleep and wake at roughly the same times each day?**

When weekdays are early and weekends drift late, you can create a weekly “time‑zone shift” without traveling. That pattern is often called **social jet lag**.

!Alarm clock

*Source: Wikimedia Commons (alarm clock)*

---

## 1) Why regular timing can matter

Humans do better with predictability. When timing is chaotic, common downstream effects include:

- daytime fatigue → more caffeine → later bedtime;
- stronger appetite and sweet cravings;
- less motivation to exercise.

Those behavior changes are a realistic bridge between irregular sleep and worse metabolic outcomes.

---

## 2) A simple self‑check

Ask one question:

> Is your weekend wake time often **1–2+ hours** later than weekdays?

The larger the gap, the more “jet lag‑like” the week becomes.

---

## 3) Minimal action: stabilize wake time first

If you can do only one thing:

- keep your wake time as consistent as possible (including weekends);
- if you need catch‑up, use a short 20–30 minute nap instead of sleeping until noon;
- reduce bright light and screens earlier in the evening.
